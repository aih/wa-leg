## 1. What a fiscal note is

A fiscal note is "an objective statement of the fiscal impact of proposed legislation linked to a particular version of a bill, draft bill, or bill amendment" (OFM agency instructions, p. 3). Three types exist: state agency, local government (prepared by the Local Government Fiscal Note Program at the Department of Commerce under chapter 43.132 RCW), and judicial (Administrative Office of the Courts).

### Statutory basis

- RCW 43.88A.020: OFM coordinates fiscal notes with all affected agencies. A note must show, by fiscal year, the impact for the remainder of the biennium in which the bill takes effect plus a cumulative forecast for the succeeding four fiscal years, and must separately identify operating and capital impacts. Bills uniquely affecting school districts also get a school district fiscal note (RCW 28A.300.0401). The ten-year cost projection under RCW 43.135.031 takes precedence over fiscal notes.
- RCW 43.88A.030: approved notes are filed with the committee of referral, Senate Ways and Means, and House revenue and appropriations committees, "prior to or at the time the bill or resolution is first heard" whenever possible, and travel with the bill.
- RCW 43.88A.040: any legislator may request a fiscal note.
- RCW 43.135.031 (Initiative 960): OFM must determine the cost to taxpayers over the first ten years of any bill that raises taxes or imposes or increases fees, publish it by press release and email, with a year-by-year breakdown and separate projections per revenue source, and update it at each committee approval and floor passage. RCW 43.135.034 defines "raises taxes" as any action that increases state tax revenue deposited in any fund or account. RCW 43.135.055 requires a simple majority vote to impose or increase a fee and references the 43.135.031 accountability process.
- RCW 82.32.808: new tax preference bills must contain a tax preference performance statement (purpose category, policy objective, metrics, data). RCW 82.32.805: a new preference expires on January 1 following the tenth year after its effective date unless the bill sets its own expiration or is exempted.

### Timeline

The statute contains no hour count. OFM practice (both instruction documents): the Legislature wants the note at least 24 hours before the hearing, so OFM sets a due date "generally 72 hours, or three days, in advance". Local government notes get seven calendar days. FNS shows the next scheduled hearing date on each request. The RFP restates DOR's operating constraint: fiscal notes and bill analyses within 72 hours, with DOR's response required at least four hours before a hearing, "often meaning the agency has less than 24 hours to respond".

### Process

1. Legislative staff (or OFM, or an agency by unsolicited email through FNS) request a note on a specific bill version, draft, or amendment. The request carries requester, legislative contact and phone, due date, hearing information, and special instructions.
2. An OFM analyst assigns agencies, designates a lead agency, and assigns an OFM reviewer. The lead agency records shared assumptions on the Request tab.
3. Each agency prepares its note in FNS (or pastes it in), approves it internally, and releases it to OFM. OFM cannot see the note until release.
4. OFM reviews for "form, accuracy, reasonableness, and completeness", may ask for a revision, and distributes the package. Distribution posts the PDF to the public site and emails a link to requesters, the OFM analyst, and the agency. "A fiscal note is not official and final until distributed by OFM." Drafts shared with legislative staff must be marked "DRAFT".
5. For subsequent bill versions, agencies create a new version based on a prior one and describe what changed on the Summary tab.

Ten-year analyses are requested only by OFM, usually together with the fiscal note request, and take precedence over other fiscal note work. FNS flags bills marked yes/no/blank for the ten-year requirement.

## 2. The package

One published PDF (one packageID, called FNPID inside the document) contains everything OFM distributed for one bill version:

1. **Multiple Agency Fiscal Note Summary** (OFM cover). Tables: Estimated Cash Receipts by agency with GF-State, NGF-Outlook, and Total columns for 2025-27, 2027-29, 2029-31; local government rows (Local Gov. Courts, Loc School dist-SPI, Local Gov. Other, Local Gov. Total); Estimated Operating Expenditures by agency with FTEs, GF-State, NGF-Outlook, Total; Estimated Capital Budget Expenditures and Breakout. Footer: "Prepared by: <OFM analyst>, OFM", phone, "Date Published", and a status word: `Final`, `Revised`, or `Preliminary`. A revised package may carry a one-line reason (HB 2227 revised: "DOR expenditures were reduced."). Packages with only one agency omit the summary and open directly with the agency note (HB 1993, HB 1376, HB 1375, HB 1320, HB 1284, HB 2020).
2. **Agency notes**, one per assigned agency, in agency-code order. DOR is agency 140. DOR's note is titled "Department of Revenue Fiscal Note" (form id `FNS062` in the footer); other agencies use "Individual State Agency Fiscal Note". The two forms differ in Part III numbering (see section 3).
3. **Local Government Fiscal Note** from Commerce when local impacts exist (HB 1318, HB 1004, HB 1100, HB 1044, HB 2227, HB 1895 with "No fiscal impacts").
4. **Ten-Year Analysis** (form `FNS066`) for each agency asked to prepare one (HB 1320, HB 1284, HB 2020 SHB).

### Labels on packages

Legiscan's supplement descriptions carry the package label. Across 5,761 fiscal note supplements in the 2025-26 session: Final 3,632; Partial 1,814; Revised 315.

- **Partial**: the cover reads "Preliminary" and agencies that have not released show "Fiscal note not available" (HB 1334, packageID 71735: OFM's note present, DOR's absent). OFM's legislative instructions confirm packages are distributed "as either preliminary or final".
- **Final**: all assigned agency notes present.
- **Revised**: a package replacing an earlier package for the same bill version. Inside, the DOR narrative opens with a note such as "Note: This fiscal note reflects a revision to the expenditures and replaces fiscal note number 2227-1." (HB 2227, 76754) or "Note: This fiscal note reflects a revision to the revenue impacts, and replaces fiscal note number 2020-1." (HB 2020, 74277). HB 1284 (72136) contains the same "replaces fiscal note number 1284-1" sentence although Legiscan labels the package Final; the label describes the package, the sentence describes the agency note.

### Bill version naming in package descriptions

The description is `<number> <version> [amendment id] (<label>)`. Version tokens seen: HB, SB, SHB, SSB, 2SHB, 2SSB, 3SHB, 3SSB, EHB, ESB, ESHB, ESSB, E2SHB, E2SSB, E3SHB, PSSB (proposed substitute), and `.PL` for the version as passed the Legislature (e.g., "2081 ESHB .PL (Final)", 96 occurrences). Amendments append the amendment identifier: "2020 SHB  2020 S HB 2020 AMH APP HARA 400 (Final)", "1376 HB  1376 AMS WM S5885.1 (Final)", "6137 SB  AMH SGOV H3681.1 (Partial)" (313 amendment packages). Inside the DOR note the bill number field renders the version with spaces: "2081 E S HB PL", "2020 S HB", "2681 E HB". The request identifier in the footer is `Request # <bill>-<request>-<version>`, for example `1993-1-1`.

### Part I: Estimates

- `No Fiscal Impact` checkbox (marked with X on HB 1126 and HB 2681 EHB).
- **Estimated Cash Receipts to:** either `NONE`, or `Non-zero but indeterminate cost and/or savings. Please see discussion.` (HB 1094), or a table. Table rows are accounts; each row shows the account title and, on a second line, the revenue source code and name. Columns: FY 2026, FY 2027, 2025-27, 2027-29, 2029-31; a `Total $` row. Decreases print in parentheses. Accounts and source lines seen in DOR notes:
  - GF-STATE-State (001-1) — `01 - Taxes  01 - Retail Sales Tax`, `01 - Taxes  05 - Bus and Occup Tax`, `01 - Taxes  50 - Property Tax`, `01 - Taxes  57 - Real Estate Excise`
  - Performance Audits of Government Account-State — `01 - Taxes  01 - Retail Sales Tax` (0.16% share of sales tax; shown as a separate small row on every sales tax note)
  - Workforce Education Investment Account-State (24J in the ten-year table) — `01 - Taxes  05 - Bus and Occup Tax`
  - Education Legacy Trust Account-State — `01 - Taxes  55 - Inheritance Tax`; also REET
  - Public Works Assistance Account-State — `01 - Taxes  57 - Real Estate Excise`
  Account codes come from the OFM Fund Reference Manual (FNS has an Account Lookup); revenue source codes from SAAM chapter 75.80.
- **Estimated Expenditures from:** `NONE` or a table with an `FTE Staff Years` row, one row per account (always `GF-STATE-State 001-1` in the DOR notes read), and `Total $`. Same five columns.
- **Estimated Capital Budget Impact:** `NONE` in every DOR note read.
- Fixed sentence: "The cash receipts and expenditure estimates on this page represent the most likely fiscal impact. Factors impacting the precision of these estimates, and alternate ranges (if appropriate), are explained in Part II."
- Four checkboxes: impact greater than $50,000 per fiscal year (complete Parts I–V); less than $50,000 (Part I only); capital budget impact (Part IV); new rule making (Part V). DOR marks the first on most notes; HB 1094 ($3,900) and JLARC's notes mark the second. OFM's instructions say the capital and rule-making boxes are now set automatically from the Capital and New Rules tabs.
- Signature block: Legislative Contact (name, phone, date of request), Agency Preparation, Agency Approval, OFM Review, each with name, phone, date. DOR preparers are the Research and Fiscal Analysis analysts; approvers seen: Valerie Torres, Marianne McIntosh; OFM reviewers: Megan Tudor (2025), Tyler Lentz, Gwen Stamey (2026).

### Part II: Narrative Explanation

Each subsection prints the form's italic instruction text, then the agency narrative. The DOR narrative uses fixed uppercase run-in headings.

**II. A – Brief Description Of What The Measure Does That Has Fiscal Impact**

Order observed:

1. Optional `Note:` line for version context. Forms seen: "Note: This fiscal note reflects language in SHB 2020, 2025 Legislative Session."; "Note: This fiscal note reflects language in ESHB 2081 as passed in the 2025 Legislative Session."; "Note: This fiscal note reflects language in EHB 2681, 2026 Legislative Session."; "Note: This fiscal note reflects a revision to the expenditures and replaces fiscal note number 2227-1."
2. Optional `COMPARISON OF SUBSTITUTE BILL WITH ORIGINAL BILL:` (or `COMPARISON OF THE ENGROSSED BILL WITH THE ORIGINAL BILL:`) followed by one or more sentences: "The substitute bill increases the business and occupation (B&O) tax rate for payment card processing from 3% to 3.1%."
3. `CURRENT LAW:` paragraphs, dash lists for enumerations.
4. `PROPOSAL:` paragraphs. Multi-part bills repeat the bill's own part headings ("PART I - BUSINESS AND OCCUPATION RATE CHANGES", HB 2081). Tax preference bills end with either "The new tax preference performance provisions do not apply to this bill (see section 2 of the bill)." (seven of the notes read) or a description of the preference's expiration date.
5. `EFFECTIVE DATE:` one of: "The bill takes effect 90 days after the final adjournment of the session."; "This bill takes effect on January 1, 2026."; "This bill takes effect beginning with property taxes due for calendar year 2026."; or the 90-day sentence followed by "However, due to the time it will take to program this bill's changes, the Department of Revenue (department) cannot implement the bill until January 1, 2026." (HB 1993, HB 1896, HB 2081). Section numbers are cited in parentheses when parts have different dates.

**II. B – Cash receipts Impact**

1. `ASSUMPTIONS:` dash list. Recurring items are listed in section 5.
2. `DATA SOURCES:` dash list, always beginning with the DOR data set ("Department of Revenue, Excise tax data"; "Department of Revenue, Real estate excise tax administration system fiscal year 2025 data"; "Department of Revenue, State Property Tax Model"; "Department of Revenue, Estate tax data") followed by "Economic and Revenue Forecast Council, November 2024 forecast" and external sources with URLs where used.
3. `REVENUE ESTIMATES:` one or two sentences in a fixed pattern: "This bill decreases state revenues by an estimated $4.3 million in the five months of impacted collections in fiscal year 2026, and by $10.8 million in fiscal year 2027, the first full year of impacted collections. This bill also decreases local revenues by an estimated $1.6 million in the four months of impacted collections in fiscal year 2026, and by $5.0 million in fiscal year 2027, the first full year of impacted collections." (HB 1318). The phrase "first full year of impacted collections" appears in 15 of 21 notes. No-impact forms: "This legislation has no revenue impact."; "This bill results in no revenue impact on taxes administered by the department."; "This legislation has no revenue impact on taxes or licenses administered by the Department of Revenue (department)."
4. For property tax bills, `PROPERTY TAX SHIFTS:` paragraph ("This legislation results in a state levy shift to other taxpayers of an estimated $3.0 million for fiscal year 2027 ..." or "This legislation results in no state or local property tax levy shifts.").
5. `TOTAL REVENUE IMPACT:` two labeled series:
   ```
   State Government (cash basis, $000):
        FY 2026 -     ($   4,310)
        ...
        FY 2031 -     ($ 12,620)
   Local Government, if applicable (cash basis, $000):
        FY 2026 -     ($ 1,600)
        ...
   ```
   Six fiscal years each. "None" or "None." replaces the local list when there is no local impact; "Indeterminate" replaces the numbers (HB 1094).
6. Property tax bills add `DETAIL OF REVENUE IMPACT FOR PROPERTY TAX BILLS, Calendar Year Basis` with four series for CY 2026–2031: State Government Impact on Revenues; State Government Shift of Tax Burden; Local Government Impact on Revenues; Local Government Shift of Tax Burden.

**II. C – Expenditures**

1. `ASSUMPTIONS:` usually a single sentence giving the affected population: "This bill affects 1,200 taxpayers."; "This estimate affects approximately 20,000 taxpayers."; "This estimate affects all jurisdictions."; "Approximately 2,000 taxpayers file an estate tax return each fiscal year." Occasionally a policy assumption or an absorb statement: "the department will incur costs for tribal consultation and will absorb them within current funding."
2. `FIRST YEAR COSTS:` "The department will incur total costs of $275,400 in fiscal year 2026. These costs include:" then "Labor Costs – Time and effort equate to 1.14 FTEs." with an indented dash list of activities, then "Object Costs - $126,200." with a dash list of object items. First-year FTEs are given to two decimals; later years to one.
3. `SECOND YEAR COSTS:` same shape. `THIRD YEAR COSTS:` and `FOURTH YEAR COSTS:` appear when the profile changes year to year (HB 2020, HB 2227, HB 2081). "The department will not incur any costs in fiscal year 2027." when zero.
4. `ONGOING COSTS:` "Ongoing costs for the 2027-29 biennium equal $9,700 and include similar activities described in the second-year costs. Time and effort equate to 0.05 FTE per year." or "There are no ongoing costs." or "The department will not incur ongoing costs."

Activity phrases recur verbatim across notes (count of the 21 DOR notes read that contain each):

- "Process tax return work items, assist taxpayers with reporting questions and respond to inquiries via email, web message and paper correspondence." (11)
- "Examine accounts and make corrections as necessary." (11)
- "Computer system changes, including contract programming." (11, object cost)
- "Gathering requirements, attending implementation meetings, and documenting and testing system changes." (9)
- "Respond to data requests and questions, compile statistics, and manage data." (9)
- "Create a special notice and update relevant information on the department's website." (8)
- "Answer additional phone calls and counter inquiries from businesses, individuals, and accountants regarding tax questions and tax return preparation." (8)
- "Amend one administrative rule." (7); variants "Amend one excise tax advisory.", "Adopt one excise tax advisory (ETA).", "Create a new administrative rule."
- "Set up, program, and test computer system changes." (most notes with system work)
- "Hear additional administrative reviews that provide taxpayers with an informal, non-adversarial dispute resolution process for the review of a disputed action by the department, such as an assessment of taxes, notice of taxes due, denial of a refund request, or tax ruling." (4)
- "Meet with tribes with revenue-sharing compact agreements to discuss changes that would be appropriate to preserve the intended benefits of the negotiated compact." (4), paired with object cost "Travel expenses for meetings with compacting tribes."
- "Investigate, assess, and collect additional taxes." / "Continued computer system testing, monitoring, and maintenance." / "Update paper tax forms and publications."
- Object items: "Printing and postage" (5), "Purchase one additional agent seat license for the Telephone Information Center." (4), "Filing fees", "Print and mail a special notice to affected taxpayers who do not file electronically."
- Property tax notes: "Revise property tax manuals, update forms and training materials, and work with county assessor's offices to implement this legislation."; "Calculate limit factors for all taxing districts."; "Provide training and policy support for county staff."

### Part III: Expenditure Detail (DOR form)

- **III. A – Expenditures by Object Or Purpose**: rows `FTE Staff Years`, `A-Salaries and Wages`, `B-Employee Benefits`, `C-Professional Service Contracts`, `E-Goods and Other Services`, `G-Travel`, `J-Capital Outlays`, `Total $`. Same five columns. Rows with no amounts are omitted. `NONE` when there are no expenditures.
- **III. B – Detail**: "FTEs listed by classification and corresponding annual compensation. Totals agree with total FTEs in Part I and Part IIIA." Columns: Job Classification, Salary, FY 2026, FY 2027, 2025-27, 2027-29, 2029-31; `Total FTEs` row. The 2026 notes use updated figures (EXCISE TAX EX 3 67,992; TAX POLICY SP 4 100,981; WMS BAND 3 114,564; EMS BAND 5 163,205; ADM ASST 5 63,246), so the salary table is a per-session reference set. Rows with 0.0 FTE are still listed (management overhead rows such as EMS BAND 4, WMS BAND 2/3, MGMT ANALYST4 appear at 0.0 on nearly every note). Some packages print `NONE` for III.B even when FTEs exist in III.A (HB 1751, HB 1924, HB 2081), so the table is optional in practice.
- **III. C – Expenditures By Program (optional)**: never used in the notes read.

The generic form used by other agencies has III.A Operating Budget Expenditures by account, III.B by object, III.C Operating FTE Detail, III.D by program, and IV.D Capital FTE Detail.

### Part IV: Capital Budget Impact

IV.A Capital Budget Expenditures, IV.B Expenditures by Object or Purpose, IV.C Capital Budget Breakout. `NONE` on every DOR note read.

### Part V: New Rule Making Required

Form text: "Provisions of the bill that require the agency to adopt new administrative rules or repeal/revise existing rules." DOR text follows one pattern: "Should this legislation become law, the department will use the expedited process to amend WAC 458-20-167, titled: "Educational institutions, school districts, student organizations, and private schools." This rulemaking would affect child care providers." Variants: "use the standard process to amend WAC 458-16-115" (substantive changes), "use the standard rulemaking process to adopt a new rule on payment card processing under chapter 458-20 WAC", and multi-rule dash lists. Left blank when no rule making is needed (HB 1318, HB 1100, HB 1896, HB 1924).

### Ten-Year Analysis (FNS066)

Attached after Part V when OFM requested one. Header: Bill Number, Title, Agency; fixed sentence "This ten-year analysis is limited to agency estimated cash receipts associated with the proposed tax or fee increases. The Office of Financial Management ten-year projection can be found at http://www.ofm.wa.gov/tax/default.asp." Checkboxes: No Cash Receipts, Indeterminate Cash Receipts, Partially Indeterminate Cash Receipts. Table: Name of Tax or Fee, Acct Code (three characters: 001, 24J, or NEW/N01), Fiscal Year 2026 through 2035, 2026-35 TOTAL; Total row; Biennial Totals row. Then "Narrative Explanation (Required for Indeterminate Cash Receipts)", which in DOR practice repeats the II.A and II.B narrative verbatim. Signature line: preparer, phone, date/time. OFM rules: only increases go in the grid (no netting of fee increases against decreases; tax netting allowed only within the same account when the net is negative); one descriptive tax/fee title used consistently across accounts; refunds not subtracted; penalties are not fees.

| Fee | HB 1044 (71440) | County technology fee $5 to $20 with CPI-U shelter adjustment every fourth year; state receipts None, local series populated; no ten-year analysis attached because the receipts are local. |
| Revised note | HB 2227 (76754), HB 2020 (74277) | Same bill version; "Note: ... replaces fiscal note number 2227-1."; OFM cover marked Revised. |
| Substitute / engrossed / passed | HB 2020 SHB (74852), HB 2081 ESHB .PL (74998), HB 2681 EHB (77479) | "Note: This fiscal note reflects language in ..." then a COMPARISON paragraph; the rest of the narrative is carried forward and edited. |

## 5. Computation conventions

Cash basis and fiscal year. All figures are cash receipts by state fiscal year (July 1–June 30). Part I shows whole dollars; the narrative series is in thousands ("$000"). Large estimates are rounded to $100,000 or $1 million (HB 1100: $498,000,000; HB 1320: $382,000,000). OFM asks for a GAAP-basis statement in the narrative when revenue and cash receipts differ by $50,000 or more in the first biennium; none of the DOR notes read includes one.

Effective-date proration and collection lag. Every revenue note states the number of months of impacted collections in the first fiscal year:

- Sales and use tax: "The department receives the taxes from monthly taxpayers the month after the business collects the sales tax from the consumer. Most local tax distributions occur the month after the department receives sales and use taxes that businesses collect. This leads to a one-month delay in revenues for the state and a two-month delay in revenues for local jurisdictions." Effective January 1 gives "five months of state collections and four months of local collections in fiscal year 2026"; August 1 gives ten and nine; July 25 with a 60-day certificate process gives eight and seven.
- B&O: October 1 gives eight months (HB 1320); January 1 gives five (HB 2081, HB 2020); August 1 with monthly filers gives ten (HB 1284). The advanced computing surcharge is quarterly and capped, so "41% of the annual tax paid is collected in quarter 1 of the calendar year."
- Credits: "The credit is first applied on the February 2026 tax return, as the department will need time to approve credit applications. This impacts four months of collections in fiscal year 2026."
- Estate tax: payments due nine months after death; effective August 1 gives "two months of impact in fiscal year 2026".
- Capital gains: annual April 15 filing; a prepayment window in October falls in the same fiscal year, so no impact.
- Property tax: taxes are assessed and collected by calendar year; CY amounts convert to FY using the April/October collection split; exemption impacts begin with taxes due the following calendar year.
- Implementation delay: when programming cannot meet the statutory date, the note states the later implementation date and prorates from it.

Growth. "The annual growth rate reflects the Economic and Revenue Forecast Council's November 2024 taxable sales and use taxes forecast" (or taxable B&O, REET, or retail sales forecast); aerospace uses S&P Global Market Intelligence; education spending uses IHS Markit; thresholds and fees index to CPI-U (shelter, Seattle area). The forecast vintage is the most recent quarterly ERFC forecast at the time of drafting (November 2024 for the 2025 session, November 2025 for 2026).

Local revenue. Sales tax notes apply "the statewide average local sales and use tax rate of 3.0%". State-shared local taxes credited against the state rate show the state loss and an equal local gain net of the 0.16% Performance Audits of Government Account share. The tribal compact sentence appears on every sales tax and B&O note: "Currently, tribes with compacts receive a share of state sales and use taxes and certain business and occupation taxes (RCW 43.06.523). Under this legislation the amount of revenue shared with tribes and local governments may decrease." with "may increase" or "does not change" as appropriate.

Population and behavior. Taxpayer counts come from excise tax data ("This bill affects 400,000 taxpayers."). Preference estimates use base amounts from external sources (Statista, Census, BLS, IRS extracts, industry surveys) times participation, eligibility, and utilization rates stated as assumptions. The Tax Exemption Study (DOR Research and Fiscal Analysis Division, published every four years; 2024 edition organized by tax type with an Excel summary of taxpayer savings by fiscal year) is the standing source for the value of existing preferences; it assumes repeal on July 1 with eleven months of first-year impact.

Property tax. Two quantities are reported: revenue impact (loss) and tax shift. The state levy is a fixed dollar amount, so an exemption shifts burden to other taxpayers with no loss while the levy stays below the $3.60 rate limit. Local districts constrained by levy limits or the $5.90 and $10 aggregate limits lose revenue.

Expenditures. Costs are in current dollars with no inflation. The first year carries system changes (contract programming as object C), rule making, notices, and stakeholder meetings; the second year carries taxpayer service; ongoing years repeat the second year. Object totals in III.A equal the account totals in Part I; III.B FTE totals equal the FTE row. FTE columns for biennia are averages of the two years (HB 1993: 1.1 and 0.4 print as 0.8), while dollar columns are sums.

## 6. Formats and transmission

- Public output is PDF only. The FNS public search page states "all fiscal notes are in Adobe PDF format". Search fields: session year, bill or initiative type, number, title; results list session, number, title, published date, and type. Direct download by packageID works without a session.
- Agencies enter data in FNS tabs (Agency Info, Summary, Cash Receipts, FTEs, Operating, Capital, New Rules, 10-Yr Analysis). Tables can be pasted from an Excel template (no blank rows). Narrative text boxes accept plain text; "If the narrative requires rich-text formatting to make it easier to read and understand, attach the narrative in lieu of entering it into FNS" (the attached "FN-2"), in which case the PDF carries two sets of tables and the system narrative should say "see attached". DOR does not use attachments in the notes read; its narratives are plain text with uppercase headings and dash lists, which is consistent with the RFP's "limited editor" for fiscal notes (B.RFA.10) against a rich text editor for fiscal estimates and data requests (B.RFA.09).
- OFM's FNS page says "some agencies transfer fiscal notes electronically (upload fiscal notes from agency system to FNS)". The RFP requires "xml transmission" to FNS and exports in "xml, word, pdf" compliant with OFM requirements. No public schema, DTD, or field list for the upload was found on ofm.wa.gov; the XML contract has to come from OFM or from DOR's current LTS. This report does not describe it.
- FNS produces the PDF from the entered fields ("Reorganizes information you provide in each screen and produces the fiscal note in the format approved by the Legislature"). Bill number, title, contacts, and agency name are filled from the request, not typed.
- Versions: a new version is created from a previous version or from a bookmarked note; the Revised checkbox is set on the Summary tab; the description of changes between bill versions goes in the Summary description.
- Release: "Release to OFM" on the Agency Info tab, available only to users with release rights. FNS user roles: Read, Author, and two further roles (approve/release) set by the agency security officer.

## 7. Fiscal note versus fiscal estimate

From the RFP glossary: a fiscal note is an estimate of revenue and expenses for a bill (including bills without a number yet) that "must be transmitted directly to an external agency" (OFM); a fiscal estimate is a projection "associated with a concept that may become a bill"; a package "can contain many fiscal estimates ... which must be combined for a particular purpose". Fiscal estimates "mirror the fiscal note process, but are informal DOR documents created year-round for multiple customers". DOR's 2024 legislative summary (as indexed) reports 349 fiscal notes in the 2024 session, 96% delivered on time, and 147 revenue and/or expenditure fiscal estimates "tied to a query instead of a bill".

Differences that matter for the tool:

| | Fiscal note | Fiscal estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | FNS request on a bill version, draft, or amendment | Internal or legislative query, sponsor letter, staff email |
| Identifier | Request # bill-request-version; OFM packageID after publication | DOR-assigned identifier (auto-assigned, overridable per B.RFA.03) |
| Form | FNS062 fields; plain-text narrative; fixed tables | Same section structure; rich text allowed |
| Output | Released to OFM; published PDF | DOR document sent by email; not published |
| Grouping | OFM package per bill version (multi-agency) | DOR package of estimates for a purpose (for example a budget proposal with several concepts) |
| Review | Agency Preparation, Agency Approval, then OFM Review | Executive Review (B.RFA.01/02) before release to the requester |
| Ten-year analysis | Attached when OFM requests | Not applicable |

## 8. Implications for the drafting tool

Structured data versus narrative:

| Data | Structured | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Bill number, version, short title, agency, request id, legislative contact | From the request/LSC | Header, footer |
| Preparer, approver, OFM reviewer, dates | Workflow | Signature block |
| Flags: no fiscal impact, >$50k / <$50k, capital, rule making, revised, indeterminate receipts, indeterminate expenditures, partially indeterminate | Booleans | Part I, ten-year header |
| Cash receipts by account and revenue source, six fiscal years | Numeric grid | Part I, II.B series, OFM summary |
| Local government impact, six fiscal years | Numeric series | II.B series, Commerce note |
| Property tax CY detail (four series) | Numeric | II.B |
| Expenditures by account, by object, FTE by job class, six fiscal years | Numeric grids | Part I, III.A, III.B |
| Ten-year receipts by tax/fee title and account, ten years | Numeric grid | FNS066 |
| Rule references (WAC number, title, process) | List | Part V |
| Narrative: current law, proposal, effective date, assumptions, data sources, revenue estimate sentences, cost narratives, comparison note | Text under fixed headings | Part II |

Values that must reconcile:

- Part I receipts: biennium columns equal the sum of the two fiscal years; the 2027-29 and 2029-31 columns equal the sums of FY 2028–29 and FY 2030–31 from the II.B series (HB 1318: 11,220 + 11,620 = 22,840). Store the six-year series and derive the five columns.
- Part I `Total $` equals the sum of account rows; the account rows sum across revenue sources.
- Expenditures: Part I account totals equal III.A object totals for every column; FTE Staff Years in Part I equals the III.A FTE row and the III.B `Total FTEs` row; biennium FTE columns are two-year averages.
- II.C dollar totals per year ("The department will incur total costs of $275,400 in fiscal year 2026") equal the Part I column; "Object Costs - $126,200" equals the sum of the non-salary, non-benefit objects for that year; the FTE sentence equals the III.B total for that year.
- The REVENUE ESTIMATES sentence quotes FY1 and FY2 from the series in millions with the month count derived from the effective/implementation date and lag.
- Ten-year table: the first six years equal the fiscal note series for the increase portion only; `2026-35 TOTAL` and `Biennial Totals` are sums.
- OFM summary rows (GF-State, NGF-Outlook, Total) can be derived from the account list; NGF-Outlook membership per account is reference data.

Reference data the tool should hold per session: fiscal year and biennium labels (odd-year session: ensuing biennium first; even-year: current biennium); account titles and codes with NGF-Outlook flag; revenue source codes; job classifications with annual salaries; standard activity and object-cost phrases; standard assumption sentences (lag, local rate, Performance Audits share, tribal compacts, forecast vintage); WAC titles for chapter 458; effective-date rules (90 days after sine die date for the session).

Limited editor scope for fiscal notes: fixed section order and headings; paragraphs and dash lists; no tables, fonts, colors, or images inside the narrative; the series blocks (TOTAL REVENUE IMPACT, CY detail) rendered from data rather than typed; spell check. Fiscal estimates use the same skeleton in a rich text editor and may add tables and formatting.

Versions and lineage: each note is tied to a bill version and a request; a revision of the same version increments the version counter and inserts the "replaces fiscal note number" sentence; a note on a new bill version is created from the prior note with the "reflects language in" note and a COMPARISON heading, and the tool should show a diff of the two bill texts alongside. The Legiscan labels Partial/Final/Revised describe the OFM package, not DOR's note; DOR's status states are internal (draft, in review, executive review, released to OFM) plus the OFM outcome (published, revision requested).

Not verified in this research: the FNS XML upload schema; the meaning of the numeric footer value on the DOR form; whether DOR's current LTS already emits XML to FNS or whether staff re-key into FNS; the exact FNS role names beyond Read and Author; how OFM decides NGF-Outlook membership per account.
